The Theme Drops: AI Slop and a Race Against Time

📅 May 19, 2026👤 By Aslı Pelinsu Öztürk

The first day of the Game Jam, we all woke up to an early morning, excited to go to our host university HdM, and meet our teams we would be working with for the event. After a short car trip, we took our first step to the beautiful campus where we would spend the next three days grinding to put together our video games.

When we finally found our way through the tiny labyrinth of stairs and corridors, the room had been almost filled out with fellow developers, designers and artists. After another few minutes, the organizing team gave their welcome presentation and finally announced the theme, drum rolls… AI Slop!

Then the Game Jam was officially launched, and all four of us separated into our respective teams. The initial moments were spent getting to know each other a little bit more, as it was the first time meeting each other face-to-face after the online sessions.

But before long, the conversation turned to the issue at hand. What were we going to do? The ideas were thrown all over, everyone suggesting different concepts and gameplay. Game design, art style and mechanics were discussed until everyone found a common ground about what the game should be.

Quickly, each person got a role depending on their skills and knowledge, most of us focusing on the coding and game mechanics, while other teammates focused on the assets and audio. Myself and Mitch had some experience with game development, while for Sebastian and Season this was a new area. Regardless, at the end, everyone found themselves with a game engine they never messed with, Godot, and the race against time began.

We have been supplied with tons of coffee and pretzels, which helped us through for the first few hours until our lunch arrived: Lahmacun wraps and Çig Köfte wraps! I was completely ecstatic because Lahmacun is my favourite dish and I have been missing it dearly since I went abroad. Season, however, was not really impressed because of the vegetables inside.

The rest of the day continued with working and chatting about what we were doing when we took breaks, which was quite often for Mitch and me being the two smokers of the group. And when we finally decided it was time to leave, the sun had already set, and everyone was ready to hit the sack after a long but productive day.

Coffee, Pizza & Progress

📅 May 19, 2026👤 By Season Thapa

The second day we were among the first people to arrive at the Game Jam site, since everyone wanted to get back to work as soon as possible. Just like the first day, we continued to work on our games and build on the framework done yesterday.

But as the game got more complex, the more issues we faced. Problems got solved, assets reviewed and changed, new suggestions and ideas made.

Just about I was beginning to starve, they informed us it was time for pizza. After stacking some slices, our group and some people from other groups went downstairs to eat and get some fresh air. As we snacked on our lunch, we chatted and laughed, recharging for the next few hours.

But since my team was making good time on our game, I decided to walk back home earlier and put on some exercise after long hours of sitting and staring at the screen.

The Final Lap: From Chaos to Submission

📅 May 19, 2026👤 By Aslı Pelinsu Öztürk

On the third and final day, we were back at the campus in the morning, rushing to finish the last parts of the game. For me, there was still a bit more to do than I was comfortable with, so after chugging a few cups of coffee, I locked in to complete the final tasks.

As the due time for submission came closer, we all focused on polishing the game mechanics and the assets. When we were in the last stretch, we started exporting and uploading to the website. Besides that we had to do a final video showing off our game for the presentation, which my team's one man army, Alex, edited.

Around that time, people were wandering around, playing each other's games and explaining the mechanics. Then, before we knew it, the time for everyone to present our games to everyone. It was so fun to watch all the games that people came up with, and the jokes they put in them. I had worked on the game Ask BOB, Sebastiaan WinDOOMz 12 AI Edition, Season Feed It! and Mitch A.S.S - The Ai-Less Space Station, which was also my favourite.

After many applause, we were given our t-shirts for the event and went outside for a group photo. We were all tired but satisfied at the end, happy with what we managed to create in the span of three days.

As we went back to our rooms, which we had coincidentally booked at the same place, we made a pit stop at the supermarket and got some things for the rest of our stay. Then everyone went to their rooms for a well-deserved rest.

First Glimpse of FMX: Badges, Stands, and New Faces

📅 May 19, 2026👤 By Mitchell Kerselaers

When we were on Tuesday, the time for FMX had come. After a quick train ride, we arrived at the Schlossplatz. After getting our badges, we first walked around and visited some stands.

Then we decided to go to the Filmakademie Projects: Graduation Project showing, and then we discovered we had chosen the perfect one because some of the students presenting were from some of our teams and people we had hung out with during the Game Jam. It was great since we got to see what else these incredibly creative people did when given more time. After that, we got to play the games they had worked on, which one was a four-player game, so it worked just perfectly.

After a few other showings, we snacked on some food and went to some stores nearby that we wanted to check out. Then we head to the (again don’t remember the location) to get our ITFS badges, since starting yesterday we would each go to the GameZone for our shifts in our Game Jam stand.

Meanwhile, we walked around the city centre and did some sightseeing. For the evening, we decided to sit at a bar and enjoy a few drinks. After that, we went to a restaurant and ate some Dürüm along with Baklava.

But we were not ready to end the day just yet, so we gathered in my room and played some board games until we were dozing off.

Behind the Scenes of the Impossible

📅 May 21, 2026👤 By Season Thapa

Around late morning, we took the subway again to attend more events during FMX week. Several interesting presentations were scheduled for the day, including a session about the VFX used in a dinosaur documentary and another about the VFX of Avatar: The Way of Water.

Since we had already watched these documentaries and movies before, we were excited to learn how such incredible scenes were created behind the scenes. It was fascinating to see the amount of detail, creativity, and technical skill involved in producing realistic dinosaurs and immersive visual worlds.

The presentations gave us a completely different perspective on filmmaking and animation. We were amazed by how much work, planning, and effort goes into it.

Wildlife, Winners, and One Big Night

📅 May 18, 2026👤 By Sebastiaan Vermeulen

Day 6 in Stuttgart was a study in contrasts, moving from the quiet observation of nature in the morning to the high-energy anticipation of the game jam awards in the evening.

We started the day at Wilhelma, Stuttgart's famous zoological-botanical garden. It is a massive, historic site, and even with a full morning, it felt like we were only scratching the surface. The weather held up well enough to see a significant variety of wildlife. We spent a good deal of time watching the pelicans preening by the water and checking out the heavy hitters of the animal kingdom. The giraffes and elephants are always impressive in person, but there was something particularly captivating about seeing the snow leopard and the black panther. Both cats were remarkably calm, though the panther looked perfectly at home lounging on a high branch. We also managed to catch a glimpse of a gorilla and a spectacled bear, making for a very successful circuit of the park.

As the sun began to set, the mood shifted from leisure to business—or at least the social side of it. We headed over to reBOOTS bar for the official post-jam wrap-up party. After days of intensive development and little sleep, the atmosphere was thick with a mix of exhaustion and excitement. This was the moment everyone had been waiting for: the announcement of the winners.

I’ll be honest: I didn't take home a prize. It is always a bit of a sting when you’ve put your soul into a project and it doesn't get the nod from the judges, but that’s the nature of the beast. However, it was a massive night for the Howest crew. My peers absolutely dominated the podium, sweeping the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd place spots with their respective groups. Seeing the talent from our school recognized like that was incredible, and it definitely softened the blow of my own loss.

The rest of the night was a blur of celebratory drinks, post-mortem discussions about what worked and what didn't, and the kind of bonding that only happens after a week-long sprint. We stayed until the very end, soaking in the last bit of the Stuttgart jam scene before finally admitting defeat to our fatigue and heading back to our accommodation. It was a long day, but a memorable way to wrap up this leg of the trip.

The Last Glow: From the Game Zone to Palace Square

📅 May 21, 2026👤 By Season Thapa

After a heavy night at the Game Jam voting party, we started the morning a little slowly before heading to the game zone exhibition at the Staatsgalerie Stuttgart, where we showcased the games we had created during the jam.

As the afternoon went on, more visitors began arriving, giving us the opportunity to present our game to new people and observe their reactions firsthand. It was exciting to see people enjoying the game we had worked so hard on, while also getting inspired by the creative projects developed by other students. The exhibition created a fun and energetic atmosphere where everyone could exchange ideas, experiences, and feedback.

After completing our four-hour shift at the game zone, we decided to relax and explore the city a bit more. We walked around the shopping mall near Schlossplatz, grabbed some takeaway food, and spent the evening sitting in Palace Square. The atmosphere there was especially lively because of FMX week, with large screens showing impressive animated videos and visuals throughout the square.

It turned out to be the perfect balance between showcasing our hard work and enjoying a peaceful evening in the heart of Stuttgart, especially since it was our last day in the city.
